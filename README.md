# Top-Down GP

A top-down F1 racing game you can play in the browser, alone or online with up to 8 friends.

- **2026 grid**: all 11 teams and 22 drivers with their race numbers and team colours
- **Eleven real circuits**: Suzuka, Jeddah, Montreal, Monaco, Red Bull Ring, Silverstone, Spa-Francorchamps, Monza, Baku, Interlagos and Abu Dhabi (Yas Marina), built from the real track outlines at real scale. Jeddah, Monaco and Baku are street circuits: walls right by the track, paved run-off instead of grass and gravel, and city blocks all around. Monaco is the narrowest and slowest, with the Grand Hotel hairpin taken at about 48 km/h.
  - **Suzuka** is a figure of eight: the back straight crosses over the road from Degner to the hairpin on a bridge. Cars on the bridge and cars underneath never touch, and the cars underneath disappear under the bridge.
  - **Montreal** has barriers close to the track all the way round, and right at the exit of the last chicane (the Wall of Champions).
  - **Baku** has the longest flat-out run, and the old town by the castle squeezes the track to about 9 metres between stone walls.
- **Practice mode**: you drive out of your team's pit box (80 km/h limiter) while AI cars are already lapping
- **Time trial**: just you and your ghost. You start at speed so every lap is timed, your best lap is replayed as a see-through ghost, and the live gap to it shows above the dashboard (green when you are ahead). Your 10 best laps on every track are listed on the time trial screen.
- **Grand Prix mode** (single player): pick your track, driver, number of opponents and difficulty, then a weekend of optional Practice 1, 2 and 3 (skip, or 1–3 timed laps each), qualifying (none, or 1–5 laps) for the grid, five start lights, and a race of 1–7 laps with F1 points (25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1, plus 1 point for the fastest lap if that driver finishes in the top 10)
- **End a session early**: in the pause menu, End session now moves straight on to the next session. Ending qualifying asks you first: you keep your best lap (the other drivers finish their runs in a few seconds), or start the race from the back if you haven't set a time.
- **Championship mode** (single player): pick 2–11 of the circuits in any order, then race a full weekend at each one against the same rivals all season. After every race you see the drivers' and constructors' standings with the points added up, and the champion is crowned after the last round. The season is saved in your browser, so you can carry on later.
- **Ghost car**: in practice (and the practice sessions of a weekend), a see-through replay of your best lap on that track drives alongside you. You can't hit it, it is replaced whenever you go faster, and you can turn it off in Settings.
- **Online mode**: room codes, a lobby, a weekend of optional Practice 1, 2 and 3, qualifying (none, or 1–5 laps), then a race (1–7 laps) with up to 8 friends. Each session's results show for 12 seconds and then the next one starts. The host can end practice or qualifying early for everyone.
- **Online championship**: in the lobby the host picks Championship, then a calendar of 2–11 tracks (click them in race order) and the weekend format (Practice 1–3, qualifying, race laps, each can be skipped). Everyone keeps their car all season and the same AI drivers race every round. After each race: the results, then the drivers' and constructors' standings, and the host starts the next round. The champion is crowned after the last round.
  - **Points**: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1, plus 1 for the fastest lap in the top 10. A player who leaves during a race is classified behind everyone still running (the later they left, the higher), and scores the points for that place. A player who misses a whole round is placed last, and scores if that is still in the top 10.
  - **Players coming and going**: friends who come back get their car back. Someone new joining mid-season gets a free car and starts on 0 points (they can change car until their first race).
  - **The host leaving**: the season pauses for everyone ("Season paused"). The season is saved in the host's browser, and **Rejoin season** on the home screen opens it again with the same invite link. If the race was running, it carries on after a 3-2-1. If the server restarted in the meantime, the room opens again at the next round. The host can also end the season from the online screen.
- **Connection indicator** (online): a dot under the minimap. Green: all good. Amber or red: it says whether it is the **server**, **your connection** or a **friend's connection**, so you know where a lag spike comes from.
- **Race start**: five red lights with a tone for each one, the grid revving, then lights out after a random pause. Press the throttle before the lights go out and it's a jump start: a 5-second time penalty. The AI react in about 0.1 to 0.5 seconds depending on difficulty, and now and then one gets a slow start.
- **Race results**: grid position and places gained or lost, overtakes, penalties, fastest lap (with its point), plus the race's Driver of the day, Most overtakes and Biggest climber
- **AI drivers** fill the empty seats (easy / medium / hard). They keep a safe gap, leave a car's width when side by side, only pass when there is room, and make occasional human mistakes: locking up, running wide, too much throttle on the exit, hitting a kerb, a late lunge up the inside. Mistakes are more likely with a car right behind them or in dirty air. Across a full grid that is roughly 2.5 incidents a lap on easy, 1.5 on medium and 1 on hard. A car stopped beside the track doesn't make them brake: they steer round it if there is room. They follow exactly the same physics as you.
- **Dirty air**: following closely through a corner costs grip (for you and the AI)
- **Realistic contact**: cars bump with real momentum, but tyres resist being shoved. Rubbing costs time, a hard punt on a rear corner can spin you, and nobody gets thrown off the track by a light touch. There is no damage.
- **Pit lane**: fast lane and working lane, a painted box for every team, garages with team colours, pit-wall stands, a paddock, and the start-light gantry over the grid
- **Handling**: braking does not take away steering, so the car turns the same whether you are on the brakes or not
- **Grass and gravel** slow you down hard if you arrive fast, but you can drive back out (about 100 km/h on grass, 65 km/h in gravel). The gravel runs right up to the kerbs (or the track edge where there is no kerb).
- **Track limits (cutting corners)**: if all four wheels leave the track and you come back on having taken a shorter way, or been quicker than is possible on the track, a warning sign pops up. Practice and time trial: a warning only. Race and qualifying: 3 warnings, then a 5-second penalty for every cut after that (added to your race time, or in qualifying to the lap you are on). Running wide or spinning off doesn't count.
- **DRS**: in a DRS zone, press Shift or Space. In races you need to be within 1 second of the car ahead at the detection line, from lap 2 onwards.
- **HUD**: timing tower with your row highlighted, position, lap, current/last/best lap, your personal best, speed (km/h or mph), gear, DRS / pit limiter light, minimap (your car is an arrow pointing the way you are going, the finish line a small chequered stripe), a "YOU" marker over your car, and an on-screen **Pause (P)** button
- **Pause and resume** (offline): after Resume the game counts down 3, 2, 1 before it carries on, so you can see where you are first (the lap timer and the ghost wait too)
- **Full screen**: the button next to the sound button, or press F
- **Personal bests**: your best lap on each track is saved in your browser and shown on the track cards
- **Sound**: your engine is modelled from the V6's exhaust pulses (every cylinder firing, pipe resonances, ignition cut on upshifts, crackles when you lift off). Choose **Onboard** or **TV camera** in Settings and press **Test engine** to hear it. Also: the cars around you (panned, with Doppler as they pass), start lights, contact, DRS, pit limiter, chequered flag, fastest lap and menu clicks. Master / engine / effects volume and mute in Settings, the sound button next to the pause button (and on the home screen), or press M. Mute is remembered when you close the game and stays in step if the game is open in two tabs. (The onboard engine needs https or localhost; on a plain local network address the TV camera sound is used.)
- **Camera**: rotating or fixed, at three distances (normal, close, far). Press C while driving to switch distance.
- **Settings** (home screen and pause menu): steering sensitivity 1–12, camera, camera distance, speed in km/h or mph, ghost car on / off, driver names, sound
- **Version number** in the bottom-left corner of the home screen (this is version 8)

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| ↑ or W | Throttle |
| ↓ or S | Brake (hold when stopped to reverse) |
| ← → or A D | Steer |
| Shift or Space | Open DRS (when it is available) |
| R | Reset the car onto the track (if you're stuck) |
| C | Camera distance: normal, far, close |
| Esc or P | Pause menu / settings |
| M | Sound on / off |
| F | Full screen on / off |

## Run it on your computer

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) (the LTS version).

```bash
npm install
npm start
```

Then open http://localhost:3000.

To play with someone on the **same Wi-Fi**, they open `http://YOUR-LOCAL-IP:3000` (for example `http://192.168.1.23:3000`).
On Windows you can find your local IP with `ipconfig`. Allow Node.js through the firewall when Windows asks.

## Put it online (GitHub + Render)

1. Create a new repository on GitHub, then from this folder run:
   ```bash
   git init
   git add .
   git commit -m "First version"
   git branch -M main
   git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/YOUR-REPO.git
   git push -u origin main
   ```
2. On Render: **New → Web Service**, connect GitHub, and pick the repository.
3. Settings: Runtime **Node**, Build command `npm install`, Start command `npm start`, Region **Frankfurt**, Instance type **Free**.
4. Deploy. Your friends open the `https://….onrender.com` link, or you can send them an invite link from the lobby.

Every `git push` after that redeploys the game automatically.
On the free plan the server goes to sleep after 15 minutes with nobody connected. The first visit after that takes about a minute while it wakes up.

## Project layout

```
server.js            game server: rooms, lobby, online championships, authoritative 60 Hz race simulation
shared/data.js       teams, drivers, numbers, colours
shared/tracks.js     circuit outlines, kerbs, gravel, barriers, narrow sections, bridges, pit lane, DRS zones, racing line
shared/sim.js        car physics, collisions, AI drivers, practice/qualifying/race rules
public/index.html    menus and screens
public/css/style.css styling
public/js/render.js  canvas drawing: track, pit lane, cars, ghost car, HUD, minimap
public/js/audio.js   synthesised sound (engines and effects, no audio files)
public/js/engine-worklet.js  the onboard V6 engine sound (runs in the browser's audio thread)
public/js/hud.js     timing tower / dashboard data shared by every mode
public/js/ghost.js   ghost car (records your laps, replays your best one) and the time trial best laps
public/js/game-local.js   practice, time trial, Grand Prix and championship sessions (run in the browser)
public/js/game-online.js  online client: instant local response + smoothing of other cars
public/js/main.js    menus, time trial, lobby, results and race stats, championship standings, settings, main loop
```

The files in `shared/` run on both the server and in the browser, so the physics is the same on both.
