# Top-Down GP

A top-down F1 racing game you can play in the browser, alone or online with up to 8 friends: on a computer with the keyboard, or on a phone or tablet with on-screen controls.

- **2026 grid**: all 11 teams and 22 drivers with their race numbers and team colours
- **38 real circuits**: the 24 tracks of the 2026 calendar, in race order: Albert Park (Melbourne), Shanghai, Suzuka, Bahrain 1 (the Grand Prix circuit), Jeddah, Miami, Montreal, Monaco, Barcelona, Red Bull Ring, Silverstone, Spa-Francorchamps, Hungaroring, Zandvoort, Monza, Madring (Madrid), Baku, Singapore, Austin (Circuit of the Americas), Mexico City, Interlagos, Las Vegas, Lusail (Qatar) and Abu Dhabi (Yas Marina); and 14 **classic tracks** not on the 2026 calendar, the most recently raced first: Bahrain 2 (the Outer circuit), Imola, Paul Ricard, Portimão, Istanbul Park, Sochi, Mugello, Nürburgring, Hockenheim, Sepang, Magny-Cours, Kyalami, Jacarepaguá and Watkins Glen. All are built from the real track outlines at real scale. Jeddah, Miami, Monaco, Madring, Baku, Singapore, Las Vegas and Sochi are street circuits: walls right by the track, paved run-off instead of grass and gravel, and city blocks all around. Monaco is the narrowest and slowest, with the Grand Hotel hairpin taken at about 48 km/h.
  - **New in version 16**: four more classic tracks: **Magny-Cours** (France, raced 1991–2008), **Kyalami** (South Africa, in today's layout: the one a South African Grand Prix would use; F1 last raced there in 1993), **Jacarepaguá** (Rio de Janeiro, 1978–1989) and **Watkins Glen** (United States, 1961–1980: narrower, with the barriers close by).
  - **New in version 15**: six classic tracks (Imola, Paul Ricard, Sochi, Mugello, Nürburgring and Hockenheim), and **Bahrain 2**: Bahrain's short Outer circuit (3.543 km, raced at the 2020 Sakhir Grand Prix), a lap of under a minute from the same start line and pit lane as Bahrain 1, at night like Bahrain 1.
  - **New in version 13**: Hungaroring, Madring, Sepang, Austin, Mexico City, Lusail, Portimão and Istanbul Park, each with its real start line and its pit lane on the real side of the main straight.
  - **Night races**: Bahrain 1 and 2, Jeddah, Singapore, Las Vegas and Lusail are run in the dark, under floodlights (lit windows in the city, neon in Las Vegas, a red rear light on every car). They are marked Night on the track cards.
  - **Zandvoort** is the hard one: narrow, gravel right at the edge, and two banked corners (Hugenholtz and the last corner). The banking presses the car into the road, so there is more grip there and you can carry more speed.
  - **Madring** has **La Monumental**: a 550-metre right-hander banked at 13.5 degrees. It is less steep than Zandvoort's banking, so the extra grip is a little smaller, but you can still carry more speed through it than on a flat corner.
  - **Kyalami** is 1,500 metres up: thinner air too, though not as thin as Mexico City's (a little less drag and a little less grip).
  - **Mexico City** is 2,200 metres up, where the air is thin: less drag, so the highest top speeds of the year (about 350 km/h), but less downforce, so less grip in the corners and longer braking. It also has the longest run to the first corner (about 830 metres from pole). Your engineer reminds you about the thin air at the start of each session there.
  - **Suzuka** is a figure of eight: the back straight crosses over the road from Degner to the hairpin on a bridge. Cars on the bridge and cars underneath never touch, and the cars underneath disappear under the bridge.
  - **Montreal** has barriers close to the track all the way round, and right at the exit of the last chicane (the Wall of Champions).
  - **Baku** has the longest flat-out run, and the old town by the castle squeezes the track to about 9 metres between stone walls.
  - **Miami** has a short pit straight, so its pit lane is shorter than the others (a pit stop there costs a little less time).
- **Practice mode**: you start in your team's garage and press **Go out** (or Enter, or hold the accelerator) when you're ready (80 km/h limiter in the pit lane)
- **Realistic practice** (every practice session, offline and online): the AI cars do runs of a few laps and roll back into their own garages, so the track is busy at the start and the end of a session and quieter in the middle (with a full field mostly 5 or 6 cars out, sometimes 4, at the quietest). You can see the parked cars through the garage roofs. Drive into your own garage and stop, and it rolls you in: the garage panel shows your session so far, your tyres for the next run, and **Go back out**. The tower shows **GARAGE** and **IN PIT**.
- **Tyres and pit stops** (the **Tyre wear** switch, on unless you turn it off): soft (red, fastest, about 6 laps), medium (yellow, about 0.4 s a lap slower, about 10 laps) and hard (white, about 0.8 s slower, about 14 laps); intermediates (green) for a damp track and full wets (blue) for heavy rain. Tyres wear with distance and with how hard you drive them (sliding and locking up wear them faster), fade slowly to 75% worn and then fall off a cliff. Your tyres and how worn they are show above the dashboard; every car's tyres show in the timing tower and the race results.
  - **Starting tyres**: a race with tyre wear gives you 3 seconds on the grid to pick (keys 1–5, or tap). Otherwise you start on the tyres that suit the race best.
  - **Pit stops**: drive into the pit lane during a race and your crew takes over: the car drives itself to your box, the tyres you picked (keys 1–5, or tap) go on in about 2.5 seconds, and it drives you out to the end of the pit lane. In a race of 3 laps or more with tyre wear on, **at least one pit stop is compulsory**: you get reminders over the last laps, and finishing without a stop is a 30-second penalty (it doesn't apply if you used rain tyres). The AI cars plan their own strategies (some start on softs, some on mediums or hards, and they stop on different laps), and team-mates don't stop on the same lap.
- **Weather** (the **Weather** switch, on unless you turn it off; off = always dry): rain showers come and go, more often at tracks where it often rains (Sepang most of all, then Spa, the Nürburgring, Silverstone, Interlagos) and hardly ever in the desert (Bahrain, Abu Dhabi, Jeddah, Las Vegas, Lusail). Sometimes it is already raining when a session starts. Light rain makes the track damp (intermediates), heavy rain soaks it (full wets); dry tyres on a wet track have very little grip. When the rain stops the track dries over a few minutes, the racing line first: two dry tyre tracks appear, then widen and join into one dry line. Your race engineer tells you when rain is coming, and when it's time for rain tyres or dry tyres again. In the rain: short, faint streaks of rain with a light grey haze, splashes, a darker track, soft puddles with raindrop rings in heavy rain, floodlights shining off the wet track at night, and spray: a smooth white veil rising behind every car, longer and thicker the faster the car and the heavier the rain (a train of cars leaves a haze that hides the car behind). You hear it too: the rain (louder in heavy rain) and the hiss of spray from your tyres and from the cars going past. The AI cars change tyres for the weather too.
- **Drafting** (the **Drafting** switch, on unless you turn it off): right behind another car on a straight you get a tow (up to about 11 km/h more at the end of a long straight); a **SLIPSTREAM** sign shows above the dashboard. For you and the AI, in every mode. The three switches are on the setup screens (and in the online lobby, where the host decides).
- **Setup screens** (Practice, Time trial, Grand Prix, Championship): the track (or the championship's calendar) and your driver show as one card each, next to the session's options. **Choose** (or **Change**) opens a picker over the page: every track, in two groups (the 2026 calendar, then the classic tracks) with filters (2026 calendar, Classic tracks, Night races, No time yet), or every driver. Each screen opens with no track chosen: the Start button says **Choose a track** until you pick one (pressing it opens the picker). A small **Surprise me** button in the Track box picks a random track without opening the picker (it lights up while a surprise track is chosen). On a phone the options are in one column, with the Start button kept at the bottom of the screen.
- **Surprise me**: the small button in the Track box, or the first card in the track picker (Practice, Time trial and Grand Prix; and the top of the host's Track list online), picks a random track, never the same one twice in a row. You find out which one when the session starts. A restart keeps the same track, and a whole Grand Prix weekend stays on one track.
- **Time trial**: just you and your ghost. You start at speed so every lap is timed, your best lap is replayed as a see-through ghost, and the live gap to it shows above the dashboard (green when you are ahead). Your 10 best laps on every track are listed on the time trial screen. The run-up starts on the straight before the line: 320 m before it, or where the last corner is closer than that, at the start of the straight just out of that corner. A track with no time trial lap yet shows your best lap there from your other sessions (PB) on its card.
- **Grand Prix mode** (single player): pick your track, driver, number of opponents and difficulty, then a weekend of optional Practice 1, 2 and 3 (skip, or 1–3 timed laps each), qualifying (none, or 1–5 laps) for the grid, five start lights, and a race of 1–20 laps with F1 points (25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1, plus 1 point for the fastest lap if that driver finishes in the top 10)
- **Qualifying start**: everyone (you, the AI and online players) lines up two by two, the front pair at the start of the last third of sector 3, so the first timed lap begins at full speed for everyone.
- **Reverse grid** (a switch next to Qualifying, off unless you turn it on): the whole qualifying order is flipped for the race, so the slowest qualifier starts first and the fastest starts last. Drivers who set no time still start at the back. In Grand Prix, Championship and the online lobby (the host decides).
- **End a session early**: in the pause menu, End session now moves straight on to the next session. Ending qualifying asks you first: you keep your best lap (the other drivers finish their runs in a few seconds), or start the race from the back if you haven't set a time.
- **Championship mode** (single player): pick 2–38 of the circuits in any order in the calendar picker (click them in race order; **2026 calendar** puts in the 24 tracks of the 2026 calendar, in order; **Clear** empties it), then race a full weekend at each one against the same rivals all season. After every race you see the drivers' and constructors' standings with the points added up, and the champion is crowned after the last round. The season is saved in your browser, so you can carry on later.
- **Ghost car**: in practice (and the practice sessions of a weekend), a see-through replay of your best lap on that track drives alongside you. You can't hit it, it is replaced whenever you go faster, and you can turn it off in Settings.
- **Online mode**: room codes, a lobby, a weekend of optional Practice 1, 2 and 3, qualifying (none, or 1–5 laps), then a race (1–20 laps) with up to 8 friends. Each session's results show for 12 seconds and then the next one starts. The host can end practice or qualifying early for everyone.
- **Find a room** (online screen): a list of the rooms on the server with the host's name, the track (or the championship and round), what is going on (in the lobby, a race on...) and how many players. In the lobby the host chooses who can join: **Private: code only** (the default: the room shows in the list with a lock, and friends join with the code or the invite link) or **Open: anyone** (anyone can join from the list with **Join**).
- **Online championship**: in the lobby the host picks Championship, then a calendar of 2–38 tracks (click them in race order, or use **2026 calendar** / **Clear all**) and the weekend format (Practice 1–3, qualifying, race laps, each can be skipped). Everyone keeps their car all season and the same AI drivers race every round. After each race: the results, then the drivers' and constructors' standings, and the host starts the next round. The champion is crowned after the last round.
  - **Points**: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1, plus 1 for the fastest lap in the top 10. A player who leaves during a race is classified behind everyone still running (the later they left, the higher), and scores the points for that place. A player who misses a whole round is placed last, and scores if that is still in the top 10.
  - **Players coming and going**: friends who come back get their car back. Someone new joining mid-season gets a free car and starts on 0 points (they can change car until their first race).
  - **The host leaving**: the season pauses for everyone ("Season paused"). The season is saved in the host's browser, and **Rejoin season** on the home screen opens it again with the same invite link. If the race was running, it carries on after a 3-2-1. The host can also end the season from the online screen.
  - **Saved after every session**: the host's browser copy is updated after each practice, qualifying and race. If the room had to close (everyone left, or the server restarted), the host's button on the standings reads **Continue round**, and the round carries on with its next session, on the qualifying grid.
  - **Friends**: a friend who has raced in a season gets a **Rejoin season** button on the home screen too (it joins the host's room again; if the host isn't back yet, it says so). The standings screen shows the room code and a **Copy invite link** button.
- **Ready and quick messages** (online): a **Ready** button in the lobby puts a tick by your name. The host can start only when everyone, the host too, has pressed Ready (the Start button says who it is waiting for). **Messages** (in the lobby, on the results and on the standings, not while driving) sends one of 17 ready-made messages, one every 2 seconds: a bubble by your name in the lobby, a pop-up like "Sam: Good race!" elsewhere.
- **Connection indicator** (online): a dot under the minimap. Green: all good. Amber or red: it says whether it is the **server**, **your connection** or a **friend's connection**, so you know where a lag spike comes from.
- **Race start**: five red lights with a tone for each one, the grid revving, then lights out after a random pause. Press the throttle before the lights go out and it's a jump start: a 5-second time penalty. The AI react in about 0.05 to 0.5 seconds depending on difficulty, and now and then one gets a slow start (rarely on the hardest levels).
- **Race results**: grid position and places gained or lost, overtakes, penalties, fastest lap (with its point), plus the race's Driver of the day, Most overtakes and Biggest climber
- **AI drivers** fill the empty seats, at five levels: **Easy**, **Medium**, **Hard**, **Extra hard** and **Expert** (in Practice, Grand Prix, Championship and the online lobby). They keep a safe gap, leave a car's width when side by side, only pass when there is room, and make occasional human mistakes: locking up, running wide, too much throttle on the exit, hitting a kerb, a late lunge up the inside. Mistakes are more likely with a car right behind them or in dirty air. Across a full grid that is roughly 2.5 incidents a lap on easy, 1.5 on medium, 1 on hard and about half that on extra hard and expert. All levels drive the same car; the harder ones simply drive it better:
  - **Hard** is about 1.5% (well over a second a lap) off a perfect lap.
  - **Extra hard** is about 1% off: rare mistakes, quicker starts, closer following, more willing to try a pass, and with a car close behind on the way into a big braking zone it sometimes moves over early to cover the inside.
  - **Expert** drives right at the limit of the car: almost no mistakes, the sharpest starts, the best attacking, and it always covers the inside when you are close behind (one move, made early on the straight; never into a car that is alongside or already there). Over a race it is roughly 5 to 8 seconds quicker than Hard. A car stopped beside the track doesn't make them brake: they steer round it if there is room. They follow exactly the same physics as you.
  - **Racecraft** (version 12): the AI race each other (and you) harder, with the same car at the same pace:
    - **The slipstream move**: a car close behind on a straight tucks into the tow, closes right up, then pulls out (to the inside of the next corner when there is room) and tries to get alongside before the braking zone. AI cars only go for a pass when there is room for the whole car on the track; otherwise they wait for the next chance.
    - **Late braking**: alongside on the inside into a corner, it brakes as late as the car allows.
    - **Fighting back**: after losing a place it stays in that car's tow and has another go at the next corner.
    - **Dummy moves**: a quick feint to one side before pulling out the other way.
    - **Pitting early to pass**: stuck behind the same car for a lap with its stop coming up, it pits a lap or two early to get ahead on fresh tyres (races with tyre wear).

    | | Easy | Medium | Hard | Extra hard | Expert |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Slipstream move | rarely | sometimes | yes | yes | best timed |
    | Late braking | – | – | yes | yes | yes |
    | Fighting back | – | sometimes | yes | yes | yes |
    | Dummy moves | – | – | – | some | yes |
    | Defending | – | – | light | yes | yes |
    | Pitting early to pass | – | – | yes | yes | yes |
- **Dirty air**: following closely through a corner costs grip (for you and the AI)
- **Realistic contact**: cars bump with real momentum, but tyres resist being shoved. Rubbing costs time, a hard punt on a rear corner can spin you, and nobody gets thrown off the track by a light touch. There is no damage.
- **Pit lane**: fast lane and working lane, a painted box for every team, garages with team colours, pit-wall stands, a paddock, and the start-light gantry over the grid
- **Handling**: braking does not take away steering, so the car turns the same whether you are on the brakes or not
- **Grass and gravel** slow you down hard if you arrive fast, but you can drive back out (about 100 km/h on grass, 65 km/h in gravel). The gravel runs right up to the kerbs (or the track edge where there is no kerb).
- **Track limits (cutting corners)**: if all four wheels leave the track and you come back on having taken a shorter way, or been quicker than is possible on the track, a warning sign pops up. Practice and time trial: a warning only. Race and qualifying: 3 warnings, then a 5-second penalty for every cut after that (added to your race time, or in qualifying to the lap you are on). Running wide or spinning off doesn't count.
- **DRS**: in a DRS zone, press Shift or Space. In races you need to be within 1 second of the car ahead at the detection line, from lap 2 onwards.
- **Sector times**: every lap has three sectors. S1, S2 and S3 in the dashboard turn **green** when you beat your personal best in that sector (the sectors of your best lap on that track; until you have one, your best lap of the session), **yellow** when you are slower, and **purple** when it is the fastest of anyone in the session. A short pop-up after sectors 1 and 2 shows the time and the gap.
- **HUD**: timing tower with your row highlighted, position, lap, current/last/best lap, your personal best, speed (km/h or mph), gear, DRS / pit limiter light, minimap (your car is an arrow pointing the way you are going, the finish line a small chequered stripe, and the pit lane beside the track as a thinner grey road, with the cars in the pit lane on it and the cars in their garages as faint dots), a "YOU" marker over your car, and an on-screen **Pause (P)** button
- **Live gaps** (races): inside the dashboard, the gap to the car ahead ("+0.8 LEC") and to the car behind ("−1.2 HAM"), measured all the time like the gap to your ghost. The leader sees only the gap to P2, last place only the gap to the car ahead. On a phone they share one line.
- **Team radio and race control**: messages from your **engineer** (weather and tyre calls, pit stops, the end of qualifying, and in a race when the car behind gets into DRS range on you: at most once a minute, not on every lap it stays there) come with a team radio sound (the click of the radio, a crackle, a two-tone chirp) and show as slim strips at the top of the screen. Messages from **race control** (track limits, jump starts, penalties) are big bold warning signs, yellow for a warning and red for a penalty, with a small RACE CONTROL tag, and an alert sound: three short pulses for a warning, two long lower pulses for a penalty. The engines dip for a moment under each of these sounds, so you hear them. Your lap and sector times pop up as before.
- **Podium**: after a race the top three stand on the podium (in the same panel as the results), then the results follow after a few seconds (or press **Results**).
- **Cars**: shaded team paint, a soft shadow under each car, wings, the halo, and tyre sidewalls in the colour of the tyres each car is on.
- **Pause and resume** (offline): after Resume the game counts down 3, 2, 1 before it carries on, so you can see where you are first (the lap timer and the ghost wait too). At "go" the picture carries on from exactly where it stopped.
- **Full screen**: the button next to the sound button, or press F
- **Personal bests**: your best lap on each track is saved in your browser and shown on the track cards
- **Career statistics** (home screen), in two tabs:
  - **My statistics**: your time played, races, wins, podiums, pole positions, fastest laps, championships and achievements across every mode, and for each track your best lap, races, wins, best finish and time played. Kept in your browser; counting started with version 9 (time played with version 15).
  - **Time played** counts only while you drive a session: not in the menus, the results or the pause menu (or its 3-2-1), not while a track gets ready, and not while the game's tab is hidden. Shown as a total ("Total time played: 12 h 14 min") and for each track ("43 min").
  - **Achievements**: 40, in groups (Racing, Championships, Race feats, Tracks and laps, Online and time). The counted ones (Win 10 races, Race on all 38 tracks...) show a progress bar until you get there. A card at the top of the screen tells you when you unlock one (while driving, a message at the top of the screen). New ones also put a badge on **Career statistics** ("2 new") and a dot on the Achievements tab until you have looked at them. The ones earned in the last 7 days come first, newest first, with a **NEW** tag the first time you see them, and every earned achievement says when ("Earned today", "Earned 2 days ago", "Earned 4 Nov 2026"). The race feats (Comeback, Rain master, Monaco king...) count in races with at least 5 opponents. The achievements you had already earned before version 15 unlock the first time you open it, with one card saying how many.
- **Sound**: your engine is modelled from the V6's exhaust pulses (every cylinder firing, pipe resonances, ignition cut on upshifts, crackles when you lift off). Choose **Onboard** or **TV camera** in Settings and press **Test engine** to hear it. Also: the cars around you (panned, with Doppler as they pass), rain and spray, start lights, contact, DRS, pit limiter, chequered flag, fastest lap and menu clicks. Master / engine / effects volume and mute in Settings (the engines start at 68, a little quieter than the rest), the sound button next to the pause button (and on the home screen), or press M. Mute is remembered when you close the game and stays in step if the game is open in two tabs. (The onboard engine needs https or localhost; on a plain local network address the TV camera sound is used.)
- **Camera**: rotating or fixed, at three distances (normal, close, far). Press C while driving to switch distance.
- **Settings** (home screen and pause menu), in three tabs that keep the same size: **Driving** (steering sensitivity 1–15, Gentle to Quick, and your own keys for steering, accelerating, braking and DRS; on phones: steering arrows or slider, vibration, touch sensitivity), **View** (camera, camera distance, graphics, speed in km/h or mph, driver names, ghost car on / off) and **Sound** (mute, master / engines / effects volume, your engine sound and **Test engine**). The X at the top closes it (keeping your changes, like **Save**). Clicking outside a box closes it too.
- **Graphics** (Settings): **Auto** (the default): full graphics. On a phone, if the game can't keep up for several seconds (frames taking too long), it switches to lighter graphics by itself so it stays smooth, and says so in a message at the top of the screen. On a computer it never switches to Low by itself: only when the browser draws without the graphics chip (no graphics acceleration) and the game can't keep up, the picture gets a little softer (sharpness 1.5 instead of 2). **High** always keeps the full picture, **Low** always uses the lighter graphics. On a phone the lighter graphics stay sharp (they just leave out some details).
- **Smoothness tips**: after a session that wasn't smooth, the menu shows a tip once if the cause is one you can change: the browser not using the graphics chip (turn on graphics acceleration in its settings), or battery saver holding the game to 30 frames a second (plug in, or switch it off).
- **Frame counter**: add **?fps** to the game's address (for example `https://your-game.onrender.com/?fps`) and a small box under the track map shows the frames per second, the time the game takes for each, the screen's refresh rate, whether the graphics chip is drawing, and a freeze recorder (how many times the picture stopped for more than a fifth of a second while you drove, the longest, and what held up the last one: the game's own work, drawing the picture, other work on the page, or waiting for the graphics side). Add **&cpu=1** as well (`?fps&cpu=1`) to try the game drawn without the graphics chip.
- **Stored tiles** (how the game draws): everything on the ground that never moves (grass, gravel, trees and buildings, the track and its markings, the pit lane and its buildings) is painted once into square pictures fixed to the ground, and each frame just lays down the few dozen in view instead of drawing several hundred shapes; on a wet track a soaked copy of each is laid over it, as much as the track is wet. The timing tower, the dashboard, the minimap and the name tags are kept as pictures too, and only the parts that change are painted again. It looks the same as drawing everything each frame, with far less work for the graphics chip. The tiles are used when the graphics chip draws the page (without it, laying down large pictures is slower than drawing the shapes, so the game draws the old way); the frame counter's last line shows whether they are on.
- **Quick menus**: the track lists draw just each track's outline, and the whole track is built when the session starts, so the track screens open several times faster (most of all on phones).
- **Smooth everywhere**: the run-off areas (grass stripes, gravel, barriers) are drawn in a much quicker way, so the corners that used to make the game jump (like the infield at Interlagos) are smooth now, on every track.
- **New in version 13.1** (fixes and smoothness):
  - **Smoother**: the cars move on in even steps on every screen (60, 90, 120, 144 Hz...), the dashboard, timing tower and grass stripes are drawn with less work, rain no longer freezes the game for a moment when it starts, and the spray behind the cars is lighter to draw and about half as long.
  - **Steady tyres**: the lines on the tyres no longer flicker as the cars move.
  - **Getting the track ready**: after pressing Start, a card with the track shows while a track is built for the first time (instead of a frozen screen).
  - **Less memory**: only the tracks in use are kept built (your statistics and best laps are saved separately); going back to another track builds it again.
  - **The race behind the menus**: about half the time it is already under way when you open the game (cars side by side, mid-pass), otherwise it starts from the grid; the picture turns with the track, not with every twitch of the car, and follows a car in a fight for position.
  - **Phones**: a thumb kept on GO stays pressed through the pause menu, and Resume works while it is there.
  - **Tracks**: gravel beds on the same side less than 60 metres apart are joined (no thin strips of grass between them), and a stray 8-metre kerb at Baku is gone.
  - **Qualifying**: everyone lines up two by two near the end of the lap, so the first timed lap starts at full speed.
  - **Sounds**: a new team radio sound for your engineer, race control alerts (three pulses for a warning, two long lower pulses for a penalty), the engines dipping under them so you hear them, and the engines 15% quieter by default (68; a level you chose yourself is kept).
  - **Pause**: no jump back at "go" (the picture carries on from where it stopped).
  - **Frame counter and tips**: add ?fps to the address to see the frames per second and the freeze recorder; after a session that wasn't smooth, a tip in the menu when the cause is one you can change.
  - **Steering sensitivity** now goes up to 15.
  - **Online lobby**: a friend's view of the host's settings no longer shows each setting twice.
- **New in version 13.2** (smoothness):
  - **Stored tiles** (a test version, switched on with ?tiles in the address; the normal way of drawing since version 14).
  - **Car pictures**: each car's picture is kept in a few sizes, made once, and the size closest to the screen's is used: the tyres stay steady as in 13.1, and the graphics chip no longer has to shrink the pictures again for every frame (version 13.1 made it do that, which cost a lot on some computers).
  - **Steady pace**: the cars move on in even steps only when the game keeps up with the screen; when it can't, the real times are used (13.1 could make the motion less even then).
  - **The race behind the menus**: the camera turns smoothly round the corners (it used to turn in tiny steps).
  - **Less work for each frame**: skid marks, floodlight posts and the drying line are put together only when they change.
  - **Frame counter**: the freeze recorder now says what held up the last freeze in plain words.
- **New in version 14.0**:
  - **Stored tiles for everyone**: the lighter way of drawing (see above) is now how the game draws whenever the graphics chip draws the page, in the dry, in the rain and while the track dries, at night, on Suzuka's bridge, on phones and in Low graphics. The ?tiles address is no longer needed.
  - **No skips in corners**: the tiles the camera will need are painted ahead for the whole area it can see zoomed right out, one each frame, so none are painted at the last moment; and the same tiles serve at every zoom while driving (no switching between copies).
  - **A smooth start**: the first tiles are painted behind the loading card (and in the first picture of the home screen), and there is no hiccup after a reset or when leaving the pits.
  - **Barriers on every track**: no barriers hidden under sand or grass any more. Where two parts of a track run close together, each part's run-off stops short of halfway and a wall separates them; the walls the cars hit are the ones drawn.
  - **Home menu**: a plain, slightly darker panel instead of frosted glass (blurring the moving race behind it every frame was heavy work).
  - **Time trial**: a straight run-up on every track (it used to start in a corner at Abu Dhabi, Monaco, Spa, Hungaroring, Singapore, Silverstone, Las Vegas, Zandvoort and Miami), and the track cards show your best lap from your other sessions when you have no time trial lap there yet.
  - **Online**: Find a room (open and private rooms), and the start waits until everyone has pressed Ready.
- **New in version 15.0**:
  - **Tracks**: six classic tracks (Imola, Paul Ricard, Sochi, Mugello, Nürburgring, Hockenheim), and Bahrain 2 (the Outer circuit) next to Bahrain 1: 34 in all. The track lists are in two groups: the 2026 calendar and the classic tracks (Sepang, Portimão and Istanbul Park move there).
  - **Tidier setup screens**: the track and your driver as one card each, picked in a picker with filters; each screen opens with no track chosen; one column on phones with Start at the bottom.
  - **Settings in tabs**: Driving, View and Sound, every tab the same size; Test engine the same size as the engine choice next to it.
  - **Time played**: in total and on each track (Career statistics).
  - **Achievements**: 40 to unlock, with progress bars (Career statistics → Achievements).
  - **Team radio**: your engineer tells you when the car behind is in DRS range.
- **New in version 16.0**:
  - **Tracks**: Magny-Cours, Kyalami, Jacarepaguá and Watkins Glen join the classic tracks: 38 in all.
  - **Surprise me** button in the Track box of the setup screens (the card in the track picker stays too).
  - **Achievements**: a "new" badge on Career statistics, the newest ones first, and the date each was earned.
  - **No squares in the rain**: on a partly wet track, the wet ground is laid over the dry ground in one go (it used to be laid square by square, and where the squares overlap it went on twice: a grid of darker lines).
  - **Smoother driving**: each square of ground painted ahead is now turned into pixels as it is painted, one at a time (the browser used to do it the first time a square came on screen, often several at once as the camera turned in a corner: a jolt). In the rain, one picture is painted each time the screen is drawn, not two.
  - **No jolt the first time DRS opens**: every car's pictures with the wing open and shut are made in quiet moments beforehand.
  - **Grandstands** no longer get squeezed into wedges or triangles on the inside of a corner, or sit half under another part of the track's grass: they are made narrower or left out there.
- **Version number** in the bottom-left corner of the home screen (this is version 16.0)

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| ↑ or W | Throttle |
| ↓ or S | Brake (hold when stopped to reverse) |
| ← → or A D | Steer |
| Shift or Space | Open DRS (when it is available) |
| R | Reset the car onto the track (if you're stuck) |
| C | Camera distance: normal, far, close |
| Esc or P | Pause menu / settings |
| 1–5 | Pick tyres (soft, medium, hard, intermediate, wet) when a tyre choice is on screen |
| Enter | Go out of your garage |
| M | Sound on / off |
| F | Full screen on / off |

Steering, accelerating, braking and DRS can be moved to other keys in **Settings → Driving → Driving keys** (click a key, then press the new one; Backspace empties it; Reset keys puts them back). Esc, P, M, F, C and R keep their jobs.

## Phones and tablets

Open the same link on a phone or tablet and the game switches to on-screen controls at the first touch (and back to the keyboard as soon as a driving key is pressed):

- **Left thumb: steering.** Two arrow buttons at the bottom left: hold one to turn (it builds up to full lock), a quick tap is a small correction, and you can slide from one to the other without lifting. If you prefer, **Settings → Driving → Steering on the screen → Slider** gives the old slider instead (thumb down anywhere on the left half, slide left or right).
- **Right thumb: pedals.** GO and BRAKE at the bottom right. You can slide from one to the other without lifting, or use two fingers. BRAKE also reverses when you are stopped.
- **DRS** sits on top of GO: slide your thumb up onto it (you keep accelerating), or tap it. It lights up green when DRS is available.
- **Pause** and **back on track** (↺) are the two small buttons next to the minimap.
- **Touch sensitivity** in Settings (1–10): higher means a shorter slide for full steering.
- **Pause** with your thumb still on GO: it stays pressed through the pause menu (tap Resume with another finger), and the car carries on at "go". Lift it during the pause and it is let go.
- Hold the phone sideways. If you turn it upright while driving, the game waits (offline) and counts down 3-2-1 when you turn it back.
- **Full screen** (home screen and pause menu) hides the browser's bars where the phone allows it. Before a session the game asks you to go full screen (with **Don't show this again**), because without it the game is squashed and some of the screen may be hidden. On an iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen and open the game from there (the notice explains this).
- **Tyre choices** (on the grid, in the pit lane, in your garage) are buttons you tap.
- **Vibration** (Android phones; Settings → Driving → Vibration, on unless you turn it off): a short buzz at lights out, a bump when you hit another car, and a light rumble over the kerbs.
- On a phone the screen is laid out to fit: a short timing tower (the leader and the cars around you), a slim dashboard between your thumbs, smaller messages.
- On a phone the race running behind the menus is drawn at a gentler pace (and not at all behind the other screens), and the menus have no frosted-glass blur, so the menus stay smooth and the track list scrolls easily. Turning the phone round waits until it has finished turning before the game redraws, so it doesn't freeze.

## Run it on your computer

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) (the LTS version).

```bash
npm install
npm start
```

Then open http://localhost:3000.

To play with someone on the **same Wi-Fi**, they open `http://YOUR-LOCAL-IP:3000` (for example `http://192.168.1.23:3000`).
On Windows you can find your local IP with `ipconfig`. Allow Node.js through the firewall when Windows asks.

## Put it online (GitHub + Render)

1. Create a new repository on GitHub, then from this folder run:
   ```bash
   git init
   git add .
   git commit -m "First version"
   git branch -M main
   git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/YOUR-REPO.git
   git push -u origin main
   ```
2. On Render: **New → Web Service**, connect GitHub, and pick the repository.
3. Settings: Runtime **Node**, Build command `npm install`, Start command `npm start`, Region **Frankfurt**, Instance type **Free**.
4. Deploy. Your friends open the `https://….onrender.com` link, or you can send them an invite link from the lobby.

Every `git push` after that redeploys the game automatically.
On the free plan the server goes to sleep after 15 minutes with nobody connected. The first visit after that takes about a minute while it wakes up.

## Project layout

```
server.js            game server: rooms, lobby, online championships, authoritative 60 Hz race simulation
shared/data.js       teams, drivers, numbers, colours
shared/tracks.js     circuit outlines, kerbs, gravel, barriers, narrow sections, bridges, pit lane, DRS zones, racing line
shared/sim.js        car physics, tyres, weather, slipstream, collisions, AI drivers and their strategies, pit stops and garages, practice/qualifying/race rules
public/index.html    menus and screens
public/css/style.css styling
public/js/render.js  canvas drawing: track, pit lane and garages, cars, rain and spray, ghost car, HUD, minimap
public/js/audio.js   synthesised sound (engines and effects, no audio files)
public/js/engine-worklet.js  the onboard V6 engine sound (runs in the browser's audio thread)
public/js/util.js    settings, key bindings, keyboard and touch input, personal bests, career
public/js/hud.js     timing tower / dashboard data, sector colours, shared by every mode
public/js/touch.js   on-screen controls for phones and tablets
public/js/ghost.js   ghost car (records your laps, replays your best one) and the time trial best laps
public/js/game-local.js   practice, time trial, Grand Prix and championship sessions (run in the browser)
public/js/game-online.js  online client: instant local response + smoothing of other cars
public/js/dropdown.js     drop-down lists styled like the game
public/js/main.js    menus, time trial, lobby, results and race stats, championship standings, settings, tyre choices, main loop
```

The files in `shared/` run on both the server and in the browser, so the physics is the same on both.
